Theory & Equations

The trajectory of a served volleyball is governed by Newton's second law with gravity, air drag, and (optionally) the Magnus force from spin.

Projectile Motion (No Drag, No Spin)

With only gravity, initial speed \(v_0\) and launch angle \(\theta\):

\[ x(t) = v_0 \cos\theta \cdot t, \qquad y(t) = h_0 + v_0 \sin\theta \cdot t - \tfrac{1}{2} g t^2 \]

Maximum height: \(h_{\max} = h_0 + \dfrac{v_0^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g}\). Range on level ground: \(R = \dfrac{v_0^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}\) (when \(h_0 = 0\)).

Air Drag

Real serves slow down because of quadratic drag. The drag force magnitude is:

\[ F_d = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\rho\, C_d\, A\, v^2 \]

where \(\rho \approx 1.225\,\text{kg/m}^3\) is air density, \(C_d \approx 0.47\) is the drag coefficient for a sphere, \(A = \pi r^2\) is the cross-sectional area, and \(v\) is speed. The drag vector points opposite to the velocity.

Magnus Effect (Spin)

When the ball spins, the Magnus force deflects its path. For spin angular velocity \(\boldsymbol{\omega}\) and velocity \(\mathbf{v}\):

\[ \mathbf{F}_m \propto \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{v} \]

Topspin (positive spin in our simulator): the Magnus force adds a downward component, so the ball drops faster after crossing the net — ideal for a jump serve.

Backspin (negative spin): the Magnus force partially counteracts gravity, producing the "floating" effect of a float serve.

Equation of Motion

Combining all forces, the acceleration of the ball is:

\[ \mathbf{a} = \frac{1}{m}\left( \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -mg \end{bmatrix} + \mathbf{F}_d + \mathbf{F}_m \right) \]

This system is integrated numerically (4th-order Runge–Kutta) in physics/serve.py. Edit the force functions there to refine the model.

Court Constraints

The simulator checks two key conditions:

  • Net clearance — ball height at \(x = 9\,\text{m}\) must exceed net height (2.43 m men / 2.24 m women)
  • In bounds — landing point must be within the opponent half-court (9 m deep)
